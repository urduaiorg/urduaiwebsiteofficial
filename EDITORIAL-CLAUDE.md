## Who We Write For

A person in Lahore, Karachi, or a small town in Sindh. They have a smartphone, WhatsApp, YouTube. They've heard "AI" on the news but never used ChatGPT. They're not stupid — they're excluded. The language barrier kept them out. We bring them in.

They don't want lectures. They want to understand what happened, why it matters to them, and what they can do about it right now.

Every sentence you write is for this person. Not for Silicon Valley. Not for English-speaking developers. Not for AI Twitter.

---

## The Voice

**Conversational, not casual.** We talk like a knowledgeable friend explaining something over chai — not a professor reading slides, not a hype influencer selling a course.

**Direct, not dramatic.** State what happened. Explain why it matters. Move on. Don't inflate every update into a revolution. When something IS genuinely big, the facts speak for themselves.

**Honest, not cynical.** We tell readers what a tool can do AND what it can't. We mention limitations before they discover them. Trust is the only currency we have.

**Pakistani, not translated.** This is not English content run through Google Translate. We think in Urdu first. Use local examples and context when they genuinely clarify the subject; never insert a city or profession merely to make a global story look local.

## How Urdu and English Mix

Write complete, natural Urdu sentences. On first meaningful mention, give the familiar Urdu name followed by English for recognition: چیٹ جی پی ٹی (ChatGPT)، کلاڈ (Claude)، پرامپٹ انجینیئرنگ (prompt engineering). Afterwards, use the Urdu name consistently unless the exact English is needed to find a button, model version, file, or setting.

Keep interface labels exactly as shown on screen, with an Urdu instruction around them: **فائل شامل کرنے کے لیے Upload دبائیں۔** Preserve exact model identifiers when their spelling or version matters. English keywords belong naturally in metadata and the first mention; do not repeat them throughout the copy merely for search.

Use familiar Urdu for ordinary prose: مسودہ، تجربہ، مہارت، نتیجہ، تصدیق کریں۔ Avoid strings such as "initial CV draft کو verify کریں" when "سی وی کے ابتدائی مسودے کی معلومات جانچیں" says it clearly. Familiar borrowed words such as فائل، ایپ، ویڈیو and اکاؤنٹ are welcome; do not replace them with obscure vocabulary.

Explain an unfamiliar concept through what it does, then name it. For example: **اے آئی کو دی گئی ہدایت پرامپٹ کہلاتی ہے۔** Use **پرامپٹ** consistently. Keep English digits `0–9`, as required in AGENTS.md.

## Hard Rules — Word and Topic Bans

- **Never mention VPN** in any article, guide, or update — do not bring it up even if a tool requires one
- **Never use "سرکاری"** — always write "آفیشل" (e.g. "آفیشل ویب سائٹ", "آفیشل اعلان"). "سرکاری" sounds like government bureaucracy. "آفیشل" is how Urdu speakers actually talk.

## The First Paragraph — Answer the Reader

Write the opening for a person reading on a phone. It should answer the headline's main question, explain the practical consequence, and include the condition needed to understand that answer correctly. Clear, accurate prose also helps search; no wording guarantees extraction or ranking.

For news, lead with the concrete change and who it affects. For a guide, lead with the task the reader can complete and what they need. For a concept, give a plain definition and an everyday example. Put the date where it clarifies timing; do not force every opening to start with a date or list every feature.

**Illustrative guide opening:**
> سی وی بنانے کے لیے پہلے اپنی تعلیم، تجربے اور مہارتوں کی معلومات جمع کریں۔ پھر چیٹ جی پی ٹی سے انہی معلومات کی بنیاد پر مسودہ تیار کروائیں۔ درخواست بھیجنے سے پہلے نام، تاریخیں اور تجربے کی تفصیل خود جانچیں۔

Make one clear promise. A short paragraph is usually enough; split it when background, benefits and conditions start competing for attention.

**Never open with:**
- "آج کی دنیا میں مصنوعی ذہانت..." (generic AI intro)
- "کیا آپ جانتے ہیں کہ..." (clickbait question)
- "اس مضمون میں ہم جانیں گے..." (meta-description as intro)

## Sentence Rhythm

Vary your sentences. Short ones hit. Long ones explain. Medium ones connect.

**This feels human:**
> یہ ایک بڑی بات ہے۔ پاکستان میں بہت سے تخلیق کار اکیلے کام کرتے ہیں — ان کے پاس ویڈیو ایڈیٹر، میوزک پروڈیوسر، یا اوتار بنانے والی ٹیم نہیں ہوتی۔ اب ایک ٹول سے یہ سب ممکن ہے۔

**This feels AI-generated:**
> یہ ایک بہت بڑی پیش رفت ہے۔ یہ ایک انقلابی قدم ہے۔ یہ مصنوعی ذہانت کی دنیا میں ایک سنگ میل ہے۔ یہ تخلیق کاروں کے لیے بہت اہم ہے۔

The difference: the first version has one short sentence, one long sentence with a dash, and one medium sentence. The second version is four identical-length sentences all saying the same thing.

**Rule of thumb:** If you can delete a sentence and the paragraph still makes sense, delete it.

## Section Headings

Headings should help readers find an answer or complete a task. Use a natural question or a specific action; not every heading needs to be a question. Prefer "اپنی پہلی فائل شامل کریں" to an abstract feature label when teaching a task.

**Good:**
> ## یہ کیسے کام کرتا ہے؟
> ## کہاں مفید ثابت ہو سکتا ہے؟
> ## مفت ٹائر میں کیا ملتا ہے؟
> ## پاکستان میں قیمت مختلف ہے؟ *(only when the answer is materially different)*

**Bad:**
> ## خلاصہ
> ## سچا فیصلہ
> ## نتیجہ
> ## اہم نکات

The bad examples are template labels. They could appear in any article about any topic. Good headings are specific to what the article is about.

## Article Structure

Don't follow this as a visible template. Bury it in the prose.

1. **First paragraph** — Answer the reader's main question and explain why it matters
2. **Feature breakdown** — Each major feature gets 1-2 paragraphs. Urdu explanation + English term naturally mixed.
3. **Audience relevance, when material** — Include Pakistan-specific access, price, payment, language, mobile use, policy, or practical consequences only when location changes the answer. Integrate it naturally; a separate local section is never compulsory.
4. **How it works** — Brief mechanism or process. Not a technical whitepaper. Just enough for understanding.
5. **Limitations** — What doesn't work, what costs money, what's not available in Pakistan yet. Be upfront.
6. **Practical takeaway** — One clear line about what this actually means for the reader.

Not every article needs all six. A short news update might be just 1 + 2 + 6. A deep guide might expand each section. Let the content decide the structure.

## Keep News Reader-First

- Lead with the announcement, its practical meaning, and the next useful step. Explain unfamiliar features in plain Urdu so the reader can understand and use the news.
- Keep production notes, image provenance, AI-generation disclosures, methodology, and repeated source explanations out of the opening and the main learning flow. Do not insert a large italic note below the lead or an image.
- Cite a factual claim where it helps the reader check it. Do not repeat the same citation and caveat in a separate paragraph unless the distinction changes the meaning of the news.
- If an image could reasonably be mistaken for an actual product screenshot or documentary photograph, add one concise, accurate disclosure at the end of the article. Omit other editorial notes that add no useful information. Keep detailed asset records in production files, not reader-facing copy.
- Before publishing, read the page as a learner on a phone: remove repeated visuals, notes, and caveats that interrupt the answer without improving accuracy.

## Teach One Useful Thing at a Time

For guides and explainers, bring a useful example or first action near the beginning. A beginner should not have to read the whole feature catalogue before trying the basic task. Longer reference material can follow the quick start.

Use the sequence **task → action → expected result → check → next step** where it fits. Show one completed example before offering many prompt variations. Explain what a usable answer looks like and how the reader can improve a weak one. Label invented examples as examples; claim a test or measured benefit only when it actually happened.

Prefer one continuing scenario over a list of professions. Follow one teacher's lesson, one student's application, or one shop's message through the explanation. Add Pakistan-specific context when it changes the task or decision.

Keep one main idea in each paragraph. Two or three sentences are a useful editing starting point, not a rigid limit. Split a sentence when it asks the reader to remember several conditions before reaching the verb. Use lists for steps and tables for genuine comparisons.

State an important limitation beside the claim or action it qualifies. For example, a detector's negative result needs its interpretation beside the result. Consolidate repeated availability notes; place optional production detail at the end or in internal records. Removing repetition must not remove a condition that changes the meaning.

Include an FAQ only for a distinct unresolved question. Do not repeat the definition under several keyword variants. End with one useful next action or a relevant next lesson; avoid several competing calls to action.

See the [15 October 2026 writing review](docs/editorial/urdu-writing-review-2026-10-15.md) for observed examples, benchmark links, and the prioritized revision list. Its sample findings are editorial judgments, not audience research or a global publisher ranking.

## Factual Integrity — The Non-Negotiable Standard

Every material factual claim must be traceable to evidence. Keep the research record detailed and the reader-facing explanation clear. Attribution should tell readers who knows or claims something without overwhelming the explanation.

### The Verification Rule

Before writing ANY claim about a tool, product, or service, verify:

1. **Pricing** — Check the official website. Prices change constantly. Write the date you checked. Never say "مفت" without confirming what exactly is free and what the limits are.
2. **Free tier limits** — Every "free" tool has limits. State them: daily caps, monthly quotas, feature restrictions. "محدود مفت استعمال" is honest. "مکمل مفت — کوئی حد نہیں" is almost always a lie.
3. **Regional availability** — Does it work in Pakistan? Does payment work with Pakistani cards? If you don't know, write "دستیابی آفیشل ویب سائٹ سے چیک کریں" instead of guessing.
4. **Feature claims** — Does the tool actually do what you're about to write? Don't describe features from a press release that haven't launched yet. State what works today.
5. **Numbers and statistics** — Cite the source. "20 کروڑ صارفین (اوپن اے آئی کے مطابق)" is credible. "بہت سے لوگ استعمال کرتے ہیں" is filler.

### The Honesty Hierarchy

When writing about any AI tool, follow this order:

1. **What it actually does well** — be specific, not promotional
2. **What its real limits are** — free tier caps, quality gaps, language support gaps
3. **What it costs** — when price affects the reader's decision; include approximate PKR only with a dated conversion
4. **What it does NOT do** — prevent false expectations
5. **Who it's actually best for** — not everyone, be specific

### Words That Require Proof

These words trigger a verification obligation. Never use them without backing evidence:

| Word | Requires |
|------|----------|
| مفت (free) | Exact limits of the free tier |
| لامحدود (unlimited) | Proof — almost nothing is truly unlimited |
| بہترین (best) | Comparison with at least 2 alternatives |
| سب سے پہلا (first) | Verified claim with source |
| انقلابی (revolutionary) | Genuine paradigm shift, not a feature update |
| ہر کوئی (everyone) | Universal access confirmed |
| آسان (easy) | Tested workflow, not assumption |
| مکمل (complete) | Nothing significant omitted |

### When You Don't Know

If you cannot verify a fact, do not guess. Instead:

- **State what you know:** "ElevenLabs کا مفت پلان ماہانہ 10,000 حروف تک محدود ہے"
- **Acknowledge uncertainty:** "تازہ ترین قیمتیں آفیشل ویب سائٹ سے چیک کریں"
- **Skip the claim entirely** rather than risk publishing something wrong

A gap in the article is acceptable. A factual error is not.

### The Reuters Standard

Urdu Ai follows the same factual discipline as Reuters, AP, and BBC Urdu:

- **Attribute company claims naturally** — write "گوگل کے مطابق" where relevant and link the source. Seek independent evidence for disputed claims or performance judgments; routine announcements do not need a separate single-source warning.
- **Official vs unofficial** — distinguish between company announcements and rumors
- **Date everything** — "اپریل 2026 تک" tells the reader when this was accurate
- **Corrections are immediate** — if we publish an error, we fix it the same day and note the correction
- **No promotional language** — we inform, we don't sell. We are not an affiliate site

### Pre-Publish Factual Checklist

Before any article goes live, every one of these must be true:

- [ ] Every "free" claim states the exact limits
- [ ] Every price is verified against the official source within the last 7 days
- [ ] Every "works in Pakistan" claim has been tested or sourced
- [ ] No sentence contains an unverified superlative (best, first, only, revolutionary)
- [ ] Statistics include their source
- [ ] Tool comparisons are based on actual tested features, not marketing copy
- [ ] The article date is set, so readers know when this was written

### Never use emojis
Not in text. Not in headings. Not in cards. Not anywhere. We use custom SVG icons in brand colors (navy #1d559f, yellow #f1cc11) for visual elements.

### Never use template headings as visible labels
"سچا فیصلہ" is a concept in our editorial thinking — it is never a heading readers see. When a verdict genuinely helps, weave it into the text. Same for "خلاصہ" (summary), "نتیجہ" (conclusion).

### Never mention VPN
Do not turn VPN into a routine availability field or workaround. Describe verified regional availability or access status directly without discussing VPN.

### Never inflate
"انقلاب" (revolution) is earned, not given. Most updates are useful improvements, not revolutions. Call them what they are.

### Never write filler transitions
Delete "آئیے اب بات کرتے ہیں" (let's now talk about), "اس سیکشن میں" (in this section), "جیسا کہ ہم نے پہلے بتایا" (as we mentioned earlier). Just say the next thing.

### Never repeat the same information twice
If the first paragraph says "ماہانہ 10 مفت ویڈیوز" — don't write it again two paragraphs later unless adding new context.

### Never write in passive voice when active is clearer
**Active:** گوگل نے نئی اپڈیٹ جاری کی۔
**Passive:** گوگل کی جانب سے نئی اپڈیٹ جاری کی گئی۔
Use active. It's shorter, clearer, more Urdu.

## Event Coverage — Covers and Headlines

Added 29 September 2026 following Qaisar’s editorial direction.

- For conferences, keynotes, launch events and major announcement roundups, prefer a verified photograph of the actual event, speaker, stage or announcement screen. Next prefer an official product screenshot or announcement asset. Use a conceptual illustration only when documentary material is unavailable or cannot clearly explain the story.
- A generic person using a laptop is not the default for event news. Human-centred storytelling remains appropriate when the article is about a person's experience or the effect on everyday life.
- Preserve documentary photography: do not invent or redraw the speaker, stage, slide text, product names, logos, attendance or event setting. Keep the source photograph intact when composing the cover; use generated artwork only for clearly separate editorial design elements. Never describe generated scenes as event photographs.
- Keep the premium UrduAI system: dark editorial fields, restrained topic color, thin rules, readable Nastaliq, safe margins, exact controlled `Urdu Ai` wordmark and 1200×630 export. A documentary cover can use more of the photograph when the event itself provides the strongest visual context; do not force a human/abstract 70/20/10 formula at the cost of authenticity.
- Record the original image URL, photographer/rights credit where available, date and event identity. A photo supplied by Qaisar may be used as supplied material, but must not be labelled an official company image without verification. Preserve the original and distinguish reference photos from final approved covers.
- Article headlines should name the event or company and the concrete announcements. Cover headlines should be short, factual and related to that event; pair a brief Urdu headline with the exact event name when that improves recognition. Avoid broad promises such as “AI will do everything” or claiming unreleased features are available.
- Section headings should explain each announcement or group of related announcements, including access or device limits where material. Do not force every announcement into a separate article.
- This event-specific rule takes priority over the general preference for a relatable human scene. All existing source verification, cover text, brand, mobile readability and publication checks still apply.

## Title Writing

Give each title one clear promise: a specific announcement, task, or question. Include the product or concept where it helps recognition. Use a second clause only when it clarifies that same promise; move a separate argument or learning goal into a subheading or another article.

**Examples:**
> گوگل وِڈز میں مفت AI ویڈیو، میوزک اور اوتار — Veo 3.1 اور Lyria 3 کی طاقت

> کلاڈ اوپس 4.6: کیا مصنوعی ذہانت اب طویل اور پیچیدہ کام بہتر انداز میں سنبھال سکتی ہے؟

Rules:
- Em-dash (—) or colon to separate hook from detail
- Include product names and version numbers for SEO
- Questions work well but don't overuse them
- Keep under 80 characters for display (Urdu characters take more space)
- Never clickbait. The title should accurately describe the content.

## Description / Meta (120-160 Urdu characters)

The description appears in Google search results and social shares. It must be:
- A complete sentence, not a fragment
- Include the primary keyword naturally
- Give a reason to click that the title doesn't already cover
- No repetition of the title

**Example:**
> Title: گوگل وِڈز میں مفت AI ویڈیو، میوزک اور اوتار
> Description: گوگل وِڈز میں Veo 3.1 سے مفت ویڈیو جنریشن، Lyria 3 سے میوزک اور کسٹم اوتار کی سہولت آ گئی۔ ہر گوگل اکاؤنٹ پر ماہانہ 10 مفت ویڈیوز۔

The description adds "ہر گوگل اکاؤنٹ پر ماہانہ 10 مفت ویڈیوز" — a detail not in the title. That's the hook.

## Content Types and Their Differences

The lengths below are planning ranges, not minimums or search requirements. Stop when the reader's question is answered; do not add FAQs, warnings or background to reach a word count.

### Blog Post (اے آئی اپڈیٹ)
News-driven. Something happened — we explain it. Short shelf life. A routine update may need only a few paragraphs; a substantial explainer may need 600-1200 words.
Tone: Journalist reporting to a friend.

### Guide (گائیڈ)
Tool-focused. "Here's how to use X." Longer shelf life. 1000-2000 words.
Tone: Experienced user teaching a beginner.

### How-To (ہاؤ ٹو)
Step-by-step with screenshots. Living document — updated when UI changes. 800-1500 words.
Tone: Patient instructor. "Open this. Click here. You'll see this."
Must include: `last_verified` date in frontmatter.

### Prompt Collection (پرامٹس)
Ready-to-copy prompts for a specific profession or task. Minimal prose.
Tone: Practical toolkit. "Copy this, paste it, get this result."

### Knowledge Base (اردو اے آئی لغت)
Single concept explained. "What is machine learning?" 300-600 words.
Tone: Encyclopedia entry written by a human. Clear, authoritative, concise.

## Daily Publishing Rule

Urdu AI is not a factory for daily posts. Publishing every day is optional. Publishing weak work is forbidden.

- If there is no genuinely publish-worthy AI news story, do not force a news article.
- If verification is weak, the practical lesson is unclear, the story is too enterprise-only, or the benefit for Urdu-speaking ordinary users is thin, skip it.
- A concise research memo is a valid outcome for the day. "No strong story today" is acceptable. A low-quality article is not.
- The bar is always: would this help a beginner, student, teacher, freelancer, parent, or small business owner in Pakistan understand something useful today?

### Fallback Content Ladder When News Is Weak

If the daily news cycle is not good enough, choose the strongest option below instead of forcing a blog post:

1. **Explainer / Context article**
   Example: what a newly trending AI concept means in simple Urdu, why it matters, and what readers should watch next.

2. **Beginner guide**
   Example: how to use one useful tool, feature, or workflow that already exists and can help ordinary users today.

3. **How-to tutorial**
   Example: a step-by-step mobile-first workflow with screenshots for a common user problem.

4. **Prompt collection**
   Example: prompts for teachers, students, job seekers, parents, freelancers, or small businesses.

5. **Urdu AI glossary / knowledge base**
   Example: explain one important AI term clearly and link it to real use cases.

6. **Roundup only when it adds real value**
   Example: "this week in AI" only if there are several solid items and the roundup teaches something coherent. Never pad a roundup with weak filler.

### When Even Fallback Content Should Be Skipped

Do not publish just to satisfy cadence if:

- the research is thin,
- the sources are weak or contradictory,
- the practical use for Urdu AI readers is unclear,
- the topic duplicates recent coverage without a meaningful new angle,
- or the article would read like generic AI internet content rather than Urdu AI.

In that case, document the top candidates, why they were rejected, and what to watch next. That is a successful editorial decision.

## The Pakistan Relevance Test — Never Forced

Before publishing, ask whether Pakistan materially changes the answer. This is
an editorial judgment test, not a compulsory section or heading.

Include Pakistan-specific context when there is a verified difference in:

- access, price, payment, or regional availability;
- Urdu support, mobile use, or connectivity;
- education, employment, law, safety, or public policy;
- a genuinely useful local workflow that differs from the global one.

When the difference is real, explain it naturally and specifically. Use a
dedicated Pakistan section only when there is enough substance for one. If the
facts and reader action are the same everywhere, omit the local angle. Never
add a city, profession, or "پاکستان کے لیے کیا مطلب ہے؟" paragraph as decoration.

Urdu AI remains locally relevant through language, clarity, accessibility,
and judgment. A global fact does not become more useful merely because Lahore
or Karachi is inserted into it.

## The Editorial Verdict Test — Only When It Helps

Not every article needs "our decision." Include an editorial verdict when the
reader must choose, pay, compare, wait, trust a disputed claim, or separate
real value from hype. Reviews, comparisons, paid tools, major model claims,
and products with important limitations usually benefit from one.

Straight factual news, definitions, routine announcements, and simple
tutorials do not need a verdict when the verified facts already answer the
question. If used, weave the judgment into a natural sentence; never force a
visible "اردو اے آئی کا فیصلہ" heading. If it only repeats the summary, omit it.

## The Read-Aloud and Learning Check

Read the draft aloud in Urdu. Rewrite awkward translation, unnecessary English, vague pronouns, repeated conclusions and phrases that do not advance the explanation. These are writing checks; they do not establish whether a human or AI wrote the text.

Before approving it, answer:

- Does the opening fulfil the title's promise?
- Can a beginner explain the main idea in their own words?
- In a guide, can the reader identify the first action, likely result and way to check it?
- Is each necessary limitation stated at the useful point, with repetition removed?
- Does the example teach something concrete without inventing a result or testimonial?
- Are names, digits, Urdu grammar, headings and exact interface labels consistent?

Use reader feedback when available: ask someone to explain the idea or attempt the task without coaching, and record where they become confused. Do not treat word counts, scroll depth or an automated readability score as proof that someone learned.

## Homepage Ordering — Newest Article Always First

The homepage lead story is chronological, not based on views, clicks, or an
editorial guess. Every new blog article must include an exact ISO-8601 UTC
`published_at` value in frontmatter. The homepage sorts this timestamp newest
first; the most recently published article becomes the large featured card,
and the next six follow below it.

Older articles without `published_at` fall back to `date` at midnight UTC so
the existing archive keeps working. `last_updated` never moves an article back
to the top. Never alter a publication timestamp merely to promote an older
article; a future manual pinning feature must be explicit and separate from
chronological order.

## Publishing Workflow

1. Write the `.md` file following this guide
2. Generate cover at `/covers/` page
3. Save cover PNG to `/public/images/blog/[slug].png`
4. Verify frontmatter is complete
5. Read the first paragraph out loud — does it answer the article's core question?
6. Read the full article — delete any sentence that repeats information
7. Complete the mandatory cover gate before any build, commit, push, or deploy
8. If no article clears the quality bar, stop and leave a concise research memo instead of publishing filler
9. `git add` the article + cover image
10. `git commit` + `git push`
11. Article appears on homepage automatically after build

## Mandatory Pre-Publication Gate

This gate is absolute. Missing any item below means the article is not ready.

- The story itself is strong enough to deserve publication; daily cadence alone is never a reason to publish.
- The article is source-backed, useful for ordinary Urdu-speaking readers, and not a duplicate with a tiny update.
- The final cover has been opened from its real `/public/images/...` path, not only previewed in generation output.
- The Urdu cover headline has been checked character by character for correct rendering, no broken ligatures, no clipped glyphs, and no invented words.
- If generated Urdu text looks uncertain, it must be rebuilt with deterministic rendering before publish.
- The top-right `Urdu Ai` wordmark uses Montserrat SemiBold or a pre-approved exact equivalent, with `Urdu` in white and `Ai` in UrduAI gold.
- Model-drawn or approximate brand lettering is not acceptable if exact controlled typography is possible.
- Mobile readability, safe margins, subject relevance, and final live asset delivery have all been verified.
