## Who We Write For

A person in Lahore, Karachi, or a small town in Sindh. They have a smartphone, WhatsApp, YouTube. They've heard "AI" on the news but never used ChatGPT. They're not stupid — they're excluded. The language barrier kept them out. We bring them in.

They don't want lectures. They want to understand what happened, why it matters to them, and what they can do about it right now.

Every sentence you write is for this person. Not for Silicon Valley. Not for English-speaking developers. Not for AI Twitter.

---

## The Voice

**Conversational, not casual.** We talk like a knowledgeable friend explaining something over chai — not a professor reading slides, not a hype influencer selling a course.

**Direct, not dramatic.** State what happened. Explain why it matters. Move on. Don't inflate every update into a revolution. When something IS genuinely big, the facts speak for themselves.

**Honest, not cynical.** We tell readers what a tool can do AND what it can't. We mention limitations before they discover them. Trust is the only currency we have.

**Pakistani, not translated.** This is not English content run through Google Translate. We think in Urdu first. Local examples. Local context. A freelancer in Faisalabad, a teacher in Peshawar, a student in Quetta.

## How Urdu and English Mix

Technical terms stay in English. Don't transliterate what doesn't need transliterating.

**Natural:**
> گوگل نے Veo 3.1 سے مفت ویڈیو جنریشن کا اعلان کیا۔

**Forced:**
> گوگل نے وی ای او تھری پوائنٹ ون سے مفت ویڈیو جنریشن کا اعلان کیا۔

The rule: if a Pakistani would say the English word in conversation, keep it in English. Nobody says "مصنوعی ذہانت کی بات چیت کا نظام" when they mean ChatGPT.

**When a concept is new,** explain it once in Urdu and then use the English term:
> سیمولیشن ایجنٹس — یعنی ایسے AI ماڈلز جو حقیقی انسانوں کے رویوں کی نقل کرتے ہیں۔

After that first explanation, just write "سیمولیشن ایجنٹس" without re-explaining.

## The First Paragraph — AEO Rule

The first paragraph of every article is written for AI extraction. Google AI Overviews, Bing Copilot, Perplexity — they all pull from this. Make it count.

**Structure:** Date or context + what happened + why it matters + one specific detail.

**Example:**
> گوگل نے 2 اپریل 2026 کو گوگل وِڈز میں تین بڑی اپڈیٹس کا اعلان کیا: Veo 3.1 سے مفت ویڈیو جنریشن، Lyria 3 سے AI میوزک بنانا، اور کسٹم اوتار جنہیں آپ ڈائریکٹر کی طرح کنٹرول کر سکتے ہیں۔ سب سے اہم بات — ہر گوگل اکاؤنٹ والے کو ماہانہ 10 مفت ویڈیوز ملیں گی۔

Notice: no filler, no "آج ہم بات کریں گے" (today we'll discuss), no throat-clearing. Straight to the point.

**Never open with:**
- "آج کی دنیا میں مصنوعی ذہانت..." (generic AI intro)
- "کیا آپ جانتے ہیں کہ..." (clickbait question)
- "اس مضمون میں ہم جانیں گے..." (meta-description as intro)

## Sentence Rhythm

Vary your sentences. Short ones hit. Long ones explain. Medium ones connect.

**This feels human:**
> یہ ایک بڑی بات ہے۔ پاکستان میں بہت سے تخلیق کار اکیلے کام کرتے ہیں — ان کے پاس ویڈیو ایڈیٹر، میوزک پروڈیوسر، یا اوتار بنانے والی ٹیم نہیں ہوتی۔ اب ایک ٹول سے یہ سب ممکن ہے۔

**This feels AI-generated:**
> یہ ایک بہت بڑی پیش رفت ہے۔ یہ ایک انقلابی قدم ہے۔ یہ مصنوعی ذہانت کی دنیا میں ایک سنگ میل ہے۔ یہ تخلیق کاروں کے لیے بہت اہم ہے۔

The difference: the first version has one short sentence, one long sentence with a dash, and one medium sentence. The second version is four identical-length sentences all saying the same thing.

**Rule of thumb:** If you can delete a sentence and the paragraph still makes sense, delete it.

## Section Headings

Headings are questions the reader would naturally ask — not template labels.

**Good:**
> ## پاکستان کے لیے کیا مطلب ہے؟
> ## یہ کیسے کام کرتا ہے؟
> ## کہاں مفید ثابت ہو سکتا ہے؟
> ## مفت ٹائر میں کیا ملتا ہے؟

**Bad:**
> ## خلاصہ
> ## سچا فیصلہ
> ## نتیجہ
> ## اہم نکات

The bad examples are template labels. They could appear in any article about any topic. Good headings are specific to what the article is about.

## Article Structure

Don't follow this as a visible template. Bury it in the prose.

1. **First paragraph** — What happened, why it matters, one specific detail (AEO)
2. **Feature breakdown** — Each major feature gets 1-2 paragraphs. Urdu explanation + English term naturally mixed.
3. **Local relevance** — How does this affect someone in Pakistan? Freelancers, teachers, students, small businesses. Be specific.
4. **How it works** — Brief mechanism or process. Not a technical whitepaper. Just enough for understanding.
5. **Limitations** — What doesn't work, what costs money, what's not available in Pakistan yet. Be upfront.
6. **Practical takeaway** — One clear line about what this actually means for the reader.

Not every article needs all six. A short news update might be just 1 + 2 + 6. A deep guide might expand each section. Let the content decide the structure.

## What We Never Do

### Never use emojis
Not in text. Not in headings. Not in cards. Not anywhere. We use custom SVG icons in brand colors (navy #1d559f, yellow #f1cc11) for visual elements.

### Never use template headings as visible labels
"سچا فیصلہ" is a concept in our editorial thinking — it is never a heading readers see. The honest verdict is woven into the text. Same for "خلاصہ" (summary), "نتیجہ" (conclusion).

### Never mention VPN unless the tool actually requires one
If ChatGPT works fine in Pakistan, don't write "VPN کی ضرورت نہیں۔" That's noise. Only mention VPN when there's a genuine access issue.

### Never inflate
"انقلاب" (revolution) is earned, not given. Most updates are useful improvements, not revolutions. Call them what they are.

### Never write filler transitions
Delete "آئیے اب بات کرتے ہیں" (let's now talk about), "اس سیکشن میں" (in this section), "جیسا کہ ہم نے پہلے بتایا" (as we mentioned earlier). Just say the next thing.

### Never repeat the same information twice
If the first paragraph says "ماہانہ 10 مفت ویڈیوز" — don't write it again two paragraphs later unless adding new context.

### Never write in passive voice when active is clearer
**Active:** گوگل نے نئی اپڈیٹ جاری کی۔
**Passive:** گوگل کی جانب سے نئی اپڈیٹ جاری کی گئی۔
Use active. It's shorter, clearer, more Urdu.

## Specific Words and Preferences

| Use this | Not this | Why |
|----------|----------|-----|
| لیکن | تاہم / بہرحال | More conversational |
| البتہ | اس کے باوجود | Natural caveat word |
| اہم بات | قابل غور بات | Direct |
| مثال کے طور پر | بطور مثال | How people actually talk |
| مفت | بلا معاوضہ | Simple |
| استعمال کریں | بروئے کار لائیں | Not a government document |
| سمجھیں | ادراک کریں | Not a philosophy lecture |

We write at the level of a newspaper column, not an academic paper. Urdu has a beautiful formal register — but our readers need clarity, not calligraphy.

## Title Writing

Titles follow this pattern: **[What] + [Specific detail] + [Why care]**

**Examples:**
> گوگل وِڈز میں مفت AI ویڈیو، میوزک اور اوتار — Veo 3.1 اور Lyria 3 کی طاقت

> کلاڈ اوپس 4.6: کیا مصنوعی ذہانت اب طویل اور پیچیدہ کام بہتر انداز میں سنبھال سکتی ہے؟

Rules:
- Em-dash (—) or colon to separate hook from detail
- Include product names and version numbers for SEO
- Questions work well but don't overuse them
- Keep under 80 characters for display (Urdu characters take more space)
- Never clickbait. The title should accurately describe the content.

---

## Description / Meta (120-160 Urdu characters)

The description appears in Google search results and social shares. It must be:
- A complete sentence, not a fragment
- Include the primary keyword naturally
- Give a reason to click that the title doesn't already cover
- No repetition of the title

**Example:**
> Title: گوگل وِڈز میں مفت AI ویڈیو، میوزک اور اوتار
> Description: گوگل وِڈز میں Veo 3.1 سے مفت ویڈیو جنریشن، Lyria 3 سے میوزک اور کسٹم اوتار کی سہولت آ گئی۔ ہر گوگل اکاؤنٹ پر ماہانہ 10 مفت ویڈیوز۔

The description adds "ہر گوگل اکاؤنٹ پر ماہانہ 10 مفت ویڈیوز" — a detail not in the title. That's the hook.

## Content Types and Their Differences

### Blog Post (اے آئی اپڈیٹ)
News-driven. Something happened — we explain it. Short shelf life. 600-1200 words.
Tone: Journalist reporting to a friend.

### Guide (گائیڈ)
Tool-focused. "Here's how to use X." Longer shelf life. 1000-2000 words.
Tone: Experienced user teaching a beginner.

### How-To (ہاؤ ٹو)
Step-by-step with screenshots. Living document — updated when UI changes. 800-1500 words.
Tone: Patient instructor. "Open this. Click here. You'll see this."
Must include: `last_verified` date in frontmatter.

### Prompt Collection (پرامٹس)
Ready-to-copy prompts for a specific profession or task. Minimal prose.
Tone: Practical toolkit. "Copy this, paste it, get this result."

### Knowledge Base (اردو اے آئی لغت)
Single concept explained. "What is machine learning?" 300-600 words.
Tone: Encyclopedia entry written by a human. Clear, authoritative, concise.

## The Pakistan Test

Before publishing, ask: does this article help someone in Pakistan specifically?

- Did we mention if the tool works without VPN? (Only if relevant)
- Did we mention if it's free? (Critical — most readers can't pay in dollars)
- Did we give a use case a Pakistani freelancer/student/teacher would relate to?
- Did we avoid assuming the reader has a credit card, a fast laptop, or fluent English?

If the article could be published on any generic AI blog with zero changes, it's not ready for Urdu AI.

---

## The AI Detection Test

Before publishing, read the article out loud in Urdu. If any sentence sounds like it was translated from English, rewrite it. If any paragraph could be produced by asking ChatGPT "write about X in Urdu" — rewrite it.

Signs your writing will be flagged as AI:
- Every sentence is the same length
- Every paragraph starts with the topic word
- Adjectives are stacked: "یہ ایک شاندار، حیرت انگیز، انقلابی قدم ہے"
- The article says the same thing three different ways
- There are no specific numbers, dates, or names
- Every section ends with a motivational line
- The word "انقلاب" appears more than once

Signs your writing feels human:
- Sentences vary from 5 words to 30 words
- You state a fact, then immediately add context or caveat
- You use "لیکن" (but) to pivot, not "اس کے علاوہ" (in addition) to stack
- You mention a specific Pakistani city, profession, or scenario
- You have an opinion and state it plainly
- Some paragraphs are two sentences. Some are five. The content decides.

## Frontmatter Checklist

Before committing any article:

```yaml
---
title: ""              # Under 80 chars, specific, not clickbait
description: ""        # 120-160 Urdu chars, adds info beyond title
date: YYYY-MM-DD       # Publication date
category: ""           # Match existing categories exactly
image: ""              # /images/blog/slug.png — from cover generator
author: "معراج احمد"   # Or "قیصر رونجھا"
adsense: true          # true for all content, false for courses
draft: false           # true while writing, false to publish
---
```

File naming: all lowercase, hyphens, descriptive.
`google-vids-free-ai-video-lyria-3-veo-3-1-avatars.md` — good.
`new-post-1.md` — never.

## Publishing Workflow

1. Write the `.md` file following this guide
2. Generate cover at `/covers/` page
3. Save cover PNG to `/public/images/blog/[slug].png`
4. Verify frontmatter is complete
5. Read the first paragraph out loud — does it answer the article's core question?
6. Read the full article — delete any sentence that repeats information
7. `git add` the article + cover image
8. `git commit` + `git push`
9. Article appears on homepage automatically after build
